CHAPTER 2

Philidor’s Defence

When a rook and pawn face a lone rook, the defender can often draw with a set-up that François-André Danican Philidor published in 1777.

White to move; Black draws

The black king stands in front of the pawn and the black rook guards the sixth rank, so the white king cannot come forward. White can only shuffle the rook about, and Black waits with the rook on the sixth rank.

The Third-Rank Defence

Counted from the defender’s side of the board, the sixth rank is the rook’s third rank, which gives the defence its name. As long as the pawn stays on the fifth rank, the rook keeps the white king cut off and Black needs no other plan.

Remember

Keep the rook on the third rank until the pawn advances, and do not let it be driven away early.

Checking from Behind

White’s only try is to push the pawn to the sixth rank. That takes away the white king’s shelter, and the black rook at once drops to the first rank to check from behind.

1.d6 ♖b1! 2.♔e6 ♖e1+ 3.♔d5 ♖d1+ 4.♔c6 ♖c1+

The white king cannot hide from the checks, and the draw is secure.
