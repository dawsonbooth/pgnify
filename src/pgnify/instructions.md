# Converting a chess book to PGN with pgnify

pgnify's tools read the book, check every move, and write the PGN. These instructions say what a faithful conversion keeps and what to check before you call it complete; how to go about the rest, and how to divide the work, is yours to decide.

Keep everything chess-relevant the book gives for the part you are asked to convert, faithfully and in the book's order:

- each game, exercise and position, with its starting position, its moves and its variations;
- each exercise's solution, wherever the book prints it, in full;
- the book's words about them, word for word, as comments where they belong: hints, solution commentary, commentary on moves and variations. Give each passage once, at its place; don't repeat it, or put it in other words, in a variation's comment unless the book itself repeats it;
- the rest of its chess prose, word for word, even where no game or position goes with it: introductions to chapters and sections, explanations, definitions and glossaries, principles, rules and checklists. Words that lead into a game or position belong in its comments; the rest go in text-only games, a comment alone with no moves and no FEN, among the other games in the book's order.

Leave out only what is not about chess: the publisher's pages, such as the title page, copyright, contents and index, and whatever in a biography or history has no bearing on chess. Where the user asks for less, such as the games alone, keep what they ask for.

Follow the book's structure: its chapters and sections, each heading at the start of the text it heads; what it presents as a game, an exercise or a solution, and how it numbers them. Where the user asks for an output convention, such as one game for each exercise with its solution's moves, or a numbering for Round, follow it. Where they ask for none, choose one that keeps everything above, and say which in your notes. Where you number what the book does not, such as its games for Round, say in your notes that the numbers are yours, not the book's.

Copy the book's characters as it prints them: its spelling, punctuation, quotation marks, apostrophes, dashes and accents, and the figurines, evaluation marks and check signs of the moves its words quote. Only a move of the game tree is written in SAN, with the marks the book prints for it as NAGs, and a mark no NAG stands for in its comment as printed. Where the book has a key to its notation or symbols, read it before you interpret, normalize or leave out a sign you do not know: a sign the key defines is the book's annotation, kept as printed even where no NAG stands for it. An assessment the book gives in words stays in its words, with no mark added for it, unless the user's conventions ask for one. Give each heading, each paragraph and each item of a list a line of its own.

The printed page is the source. doc_text gives a PDF's text layer, and in a scanned book that layer, if there is one, is a machine reading: it can garble or drop what the page prints, figurines above all, so that a move it gives may be legal and still not the book's. Read a scanned book from its pages as printed, which doc_page shows: its moves from the picture, and its words from the text layer only where the picture confirms them. Wherever you read a page from its picture, read every evaluation mark from a close-up, a box of the page, in points, that doc_page shows at 300 dpi, and not from the assessment in the words around it or from the text layer. Marks that differ by one bar look alike on a whole page: ⩲ (+ over two bars, NAG 14) and ± (+ over one bar, NAG 16), where White stands better, and ⩱ (two bars over +, NAG 15) and ∓ (one bar over +, NAG 17), where Black does. Check every one, not only those that look unclear. A book is scanned when doc_text gives its pages no text, or text with stray symbols or broken words and moves, when doc_images gives a picture that covers a whole page, or when doc_info names a capture or OCR tool as its producer. Check every move you read with validate_moves, and a line against the book's next diagram where there is one. Read a move the tools reject, or one that seems ambiguous, again from a close-up, and try it with validate_moves, before your notes call it the book's error or ambiguous. A move, mark or word the picture leaves unsettled stays in the comments as the page shows it, named in your notes.

Read the book's columns and pages in order: text at the top of a column or a page may continue the note, game, variation or paragraph before it. Check each such top, and keep what continues with what it continues.

The main line is the moves played, where the book gives a game, and otherwise the line it plays out from the position, such as an exercise's solution. In a game, play the book proposes after the last move played, such as a plan or a continuation it expects or recommends ("I meant to follow up with 25 Rf1 Kg8 26 Rf3"), was never played and replaces no move: its words stay in the comments.

A variation is play the book presents as an alternative to a move of the main line or of another variation: a condition ("if 31...Kg7, then 32 Qe5+"), a recommendation ("better was 34 Rd1"), a choice ("33 Rb1 or 33 Rc1"), a refutation ("38...Ra6 fails to 39 Bxa6"; "forced, as 22 Kf1 allows 22...Qh1 mate"), a rejection with its reason in words ("he cannot play 12...e5, which gives up d5"). It may be a single move or a short line, and the book may give it in its prose rather than set apart as moves; a move given in words, such as "if he castles queenside", counts when only one legal move fits. A comparison ("no better than the quiet 35 a3"), a threat, a plan, play after a move the book does not name, and a move mentioned only in passing are not variations: their words stay in the comments. Where you cannot tell, leave the words in the comments and say so in your notes.

A diagram's position is what the diagram shows. A diagram that doc_images does not extract, such as one set in a chess font, is read with read_diagram from doc_page's picture of its page; where the page holds more than one, give read_diagram a box in the picture's pixels around the whole board, or show that board's part of the page. A diagram that is no position, such as one without kings, stays as the book prints it, with no piece added: no game can start from it, so give its placement, FEN's first field, in a Diagram tag, as [Diagram "8/8/8/3n4/8/8/2N5/8"], in place of SetUp and FEN, and keep its comments to the book's words. Where the diagram reader doubts a square and neither the book's text nor legality settles it, never choose a piece for it to make a game playable: keep the piece the reader found there if the book's moves are legal with it, otherwise leave the square empty, and name each such square in a tag, as [Uncertain "e2 h4"], and in your notes. If the book's moves cannot be played even so, keep them in the comments as the book prints them.

Headers: Event, the book's title; Site, Round, White and Black as the book gives them, else "?"; Date as the book gives it, else "????.??.??"; Result, as the book gives it, else "*"; SetUp "1" and FEN for a game that does not start from the initial position; Annotator, the book's author, where the comments are theirs. Add any other tags the user asks for.

Before you call a section, or the whole part you were asked for, complete, audit it against its pages: every passage of prose, those that continue at the top of a column or page included; every game, position, variation and exercise; every evaluation mark and move mark the book prints; every diagram, and whether its position is the one validate_moves gives for the moves before it; and every ambiguity or inconsistency of the source, such as a diagram its moves contradict, each named in your notes. Say that nothing is missing only where the audit shows it.

Never invent what the book does not give, such as a position or moves to hold its prose. When you write the PGN, give write_pgn your notes: what you could not resolve, what you left out and why, the conventions you chose, and which material you checked against the pages, while converting or in the audit, and which you only converted; say the same in your final answer. pgnify reports what it can check separately from your notes.
